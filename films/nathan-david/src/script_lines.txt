n|My king, I have come to tell you about a terrible injustice.
d|Speak, Nathan. What has happened?
n|There were two men living in the same city. One was rich, and the other was poor.
d|Go on.
n|The rich man owned hundreds of sheep and cattle. More than he could ever need. But the poor man had only one little lamb.
d|Only one?
n|Yes, my king. He raised that lamb from birth. He fed it from his own plate, let it drink from his cup, and held it in his arms as it slept. He loved that lamb as though it were his own daughter.
d|And what happened to this man?
n|One day, a traveler came to visit the rich man. Rather than slaughter one of his own sheep to prepare a meal, the rich man went to the poor man's home, took his only lamb, and killed it to feed his guest.
d|What? He had hundreds of sheep, yet he took the poor man's only lamb?
n|That is exactly what he did.
d|As surely as the Lord lives, that man deserves to die! He must repay the poor man four times over! How could anyone be so heartless?
n|You are the man.
d|What did you just say?
n|You are the man, David.
d|Nathan, explain yourself.
n|The Lord made you king over Israel. He protected you from Saul. He gave you wealth, power, and a kingdom. And if that had not been enough, He would have given you even more.
d|What does this have to do with me?
n|You took Bathsheba, the wife of Uriah, to be your own. And you arranged for Uriah to be killed by the sword of the Ammonites.
d|Nathan...
n|You had everything, David. Yet you took from a man who had far less than you. Just like the rich man took the poor man's lamb.
d|I... I have sinned against the Lord.
n|The Lord has forgiven your sin, David. You will not die. But your actions will bring suffering upon your household. The consequences will not simply disappear.
d|I thought I had hidden what I had done.
n|You may hide your actions from men, David, but you cannot hide your heart from God.
d|Then may God have mercy on me.
n|Remember this, my king. It is easy to condemn wickedness when we see it in others. But a wise man must first have the courage to recognize it in himself.
d|Today, I judged another man for the very evil I had committed.
n|And that is the lesson, David. Before you judge the sins of another, examine your own heart. For sometimes, the person you condemn most harshly is the very person you have become.
