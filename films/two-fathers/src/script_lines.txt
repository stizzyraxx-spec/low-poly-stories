n|An atheist and a pastor walk into a bar.
n|The atheist orders whiskey. The pastor orders water.
n|The atheist laughs.
a|You know, for a man who believes in eternal life, you sure seem afraid to enjoy this one.
n|The pastor smiles.
p|And for a man who believes this life is all there is, you sure seem determined to forget it.
n|The atheist stops laughing.
n|After a moment, he asks the pastor a question.
a|If your God is so good, why does He allow innocent children to suffer?
n|The pastor looks down at his glass.
p|I don't know why every child suffers.
n|The atheist smirks.
a|That's the problem with religion. You always have an answer until the question becomes difficult.
n|The pastor replies.
p|No. The problem with some religious people is that they pretend faith means having every answer.
n|The atheist takes another drink.
a|Then tell me what faith actually is.
n|The pastor thinks for a moment.
p|Faith is not pretending the darkness doesn't exist. It's believing the darkness doesn't have the final word.
n|The atheist shakes his head.
a|That's beautiful. But beauty doesn't make something true.
n|The pastor nods.
p|You're right.
n|For the first time, the atheist looks surprised.
n|The pastor continues.
p|And pain doesn't make something false.
n|Silence settles between them.
n|The atheist stares into his glass.
a|I used to believe in God, you know.
n|The pastor says nothing.
a|I prayed every night when my daughter got sick. I begged Him. I promised Him everything. I would have given my life for hers.
n|His voice begins to break.
a|She was seven.
n|The pastor slowly sets down his water.
a|And when we buried her, everyone told me God had a plan.
n|The atheist looks directly at him.
a|Tell me, Pastor. What kind of plan requires a seven-year-old girl to die?
n|The pastor's eyes fill with tears.
p|I would never tell a grieving father that I knew why his daughter died.
a|Then what would you tell him?
n|The pastor pauses.
p|That when Jesus stood at the tomb of Lazarus, knowing He was about to raise him from the dead, He still wept.
n|The atheist looks away.
a|Why?
p|Because knowing resurrection was coming didn't make death any less heartbreaking.
n|Neither man speaks for several minutes.
n|Finally, the atheist whispers.
a|I don't hate God because I know He doesn't exist.
n|The pastor waits.
a|I hate the possibility that He does exist, and He watched me bury my daughter.
n|The pastor nods slowly.
p|That's a much harder question than whether God exists.
n|The atheist wipes his eyes.
a|So what am I supposed to do? Believe again?
p|No. I'm not going to ask you to pretend you're convinced.
a|Then what?
n|The pastor looks toward the door.
p|Go home. Remember your daughter. Love her without apologizing for how much it hurts. And if you ever decide to pray again, don't give God the words you think a pastor wants to hear. Give Him the ones you've been afraid to say.
n|The atheist looks at his empty glass.
a|You think God can handle my anger?
n|The pastor answers.
p|If He is God, your anger cannot destroy Him. And if He is good, your grief will not surprise Him.
n|The atheist stands to leave.
n|At the door, he turns around.
a|Pastor, did you ever lose someone you prayed God would save?
n|The pastor reaches into his wallet and pulls out a faded photograph of a young boy.
p|My son. He was nine.
n|The atheist stares at the picture.
a|And you still believe?
n|The pastor looks at the photograph for a long time.
p|Some days, I believe with confidence. Other days, all I can manage is to ask God why. But I learned something after burying him.
a|What's that?
n|The pastor folds the photograph carefully.
p|I used to think faith meant never questioning God. Now I think faith sometimes means refusing to stop talking to Him, even when all you have left are questions.
n|The atheist walks back to the table and sits down.
n|He doesn't order another whiskey.
n|The pastor doesn't offer another sermon.
n|And for the next two hours, two fathers sit together, speaking about the children they loved.
n|One believes in God.
n|The other doesn't know what he believes anymore.
n|But when the bartender comes to close, he notices something strange.
n|The atheist is praying.
n|And the pastor is crying.
n|Because sometimes the deepest conversations about God don't end with someone winning an argument.
n|Sometimes they end with two broken people realizing that the person across from them understands their pain.
